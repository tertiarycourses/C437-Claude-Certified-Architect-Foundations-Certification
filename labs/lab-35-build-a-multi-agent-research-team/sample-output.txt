# Impact of AI on Creative Industries: Evidence Synthesis

Evidence on AI in creative industries is thin and mostly self-reported or vendor-sourced. Adoption is the best-documented theme, with figures from 37% to 78% across music, writing, film, visual arts and design, but these measure different things and populations and are not directly comparable. Measured economic harm, legal outcomes and film/TV labor evidence are not established by this research. Games, advertising and design returned no usable evidence.

- **music**: In an unverified survey dated 2026-03-19 (publisher unnamed, method and sample size not stated), 62% of surveyed independent labels reported using generative AI tools in music production during 2025. (https://example.org/northbridge-label-survey-2026)
- **music**: In a self-selected member poll dated 2025-09-02 (not representative of musicians generally), 48% of member musicians reported using AI tools for composition or mixing at least once a month. (https://example.org/session-players-guild-poll)
- **writing**: In a self-reported income study dated 2026-01-14 (sample size not visible; AI attribution respondent-reported), median freelance copywriting income fell 11% year over year, and 6 in 10 respondents blamed AI drafting tools. (https://example.org/freelance-writers-collective-income)
- **writing**: In a small-sample tracker dated 2026-04-02 (25 publishers, low for generalization), 19 of 25 surveyed book publishers require authors to disclose AI assistance. (https://example.org/lanternhouse-ai-policy-tracker)
- **film/TV**: In a partial excerpt dated 2026-06-08 (publisher and methodology unverified), 44% of independent film productions reported using AI for visual-effects cleanup in 2025. (https://example.org/indie-screen-council-vfx)
- **visual arts**: In an excerpt dated 2026-05-12 from a self-selected membership flash poll, 37% of member illustrators reported losing at least one commission to AI image tools in 2025; this measures reported losses, not adoption. (https://example.org/illustrators-circle-flash-poll)
- **visual arts**: In a survey-based excerpt dated 2026-02-20 (sample unchecked), 71% of graphic design studios reported using AI image generation for concept work. (https://example.org/design-studio-pulse-2026)
- **cross-sector**: In a peer-reviewed study dated 2026-03-15 (stratified random sample of 3,100 firms in 12 countries; extract states no limitations), generative AI adoption among creative small firms was 41% in 2025, up from 23% in 2023. (Halden-Rao-2026-Journal-of-Creative-Economies.pdf, p.112)
- **cross-sector**: In a vendor benchmark dated 2026-06-01 by an AI image tool seller (opt-in poll of 640 of its own customers; vendor interest not disclosed), 78% of creative studios reported using generative AI every week. (BrightCanvas-Customer-Benchmark-2026.pdf, p.3)

- Games, advertising and design: both searches returned empty; no usable evidence exists in this research.
- Legal and policy landscape: not established by this research (training-data lawsuits, licensing, legislation unresearched).
- Film/TV labor (WGA/SAG-AFTRA terms, likeness rights, displacement): not established; searches timed out twice.
- Music, writing, visual arts: royalties, lawsuits, displacement and income beyond small polls are largely unresearched; no measured economic impact at scale.
- Conflict: 41% (any adoption, peer-reviewed, n=3,100) and 78% (weekly use, vendor opt-in, n=640) measure different things and populations; do not compare directly.
- Conflict: 62% label production use vs 48% musician monthly composition/mixing, and 71% design concept work vs 78% weekly studio use, differ in unit, frequency, task and population.
- Conflict: 37% illustrator commission losses measure reported losses, not adoption; the 11% income decline is self-reported and AI-attributed, with no link to measured earnings.
- Sources: example.org URLs name no publisher; Halden-Rao title and authors are absent from the extract; PDF scope beyond adoption is unclear. Treat as unverified.
